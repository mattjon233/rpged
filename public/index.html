    01

    01 · The Game Master

    It narrates, plays every role and never drops the thread.

    The Game Master is an AI built on Claude, Anthropic's model, seated at the same table as the players. It runs the whole scene, from the mood to the last shot, and reacts in real time to what the table decides.

    game masterexample
    1. narrate The rain picks up. The arcade sign flickers twice and goes dark.caption on the map, read aloud
    2. speak “You've got ten minutes. After that, I don't know any of you.”as Celeste, in her own voice
    3. whisper Only you notice the camera turning toward the door.only Nara sees this
    4. ask for a roll Ravi, Perception against difficulty 15.the die rolls on the server
    5. soundtrack Tension, fading in for everyone.
    6. map Open the back door and lift the fog from the office.
    7. note The crew owes Celeste a favor.comes back next session

    • Voice and captions

      Every line of narration shows up as a caption over the map and is read aloud in each player's browser. For now, the voice speaks Brazilian Portuguese.

    • A voice for every NPC

      Each NPC speaks under its own name and in its own voice. You can tell who is talking without looking.

    • Whispers and secrets

      The GM talks to one player in private, rolls in secret and shows each person only what their character knows.

    • It teaches as you play

      No homework. Each rule is explained the first time the story needs it.

    • Talk instead of typing

      Hit the mic, in Chrome or Edge, and say what your character does. Your speech is transcribed and sent to the table.

    • A world that keeps moving

      No rails and no scripted ending. The opposition acts on its own logic and the world responds to the table's choices.

    Memory

    The campaign picks up exactly where it left off.

    Three layers of memory keep the story consistent across weeks or months of play.

    Layer 1

    Chronicle

    What matters, written down by the GM itself: decisions, debts, promises and who did what.

    Layer 2

    Session diary

    Everything that was said and rolled, saved automatically and searchable later.

    Layer 3

    Long-term memory

    An index of past sessions, which the GM checks against the chronicle before it narrates.

    Split party

    The party split up? Each scene gets its own Game Master.

    When the table splits, parallel AI agents take over. Each group plays at its own pace, at the same time, and its GM hands a summary back to the main GM when the scene ends.

    • One GM for each group
    • One GM just for whoever is inside the network
    • On a day off, one GM per player

    03

    03 · Mechanics

    The rules run themselves. You just play.

    Every roll happens on the server and every rule applies the same way to everyone. No arguing over the math, no fudged dice.

    Try it

    3D dice that can't lie

    Every roll throws real polyhedral dice, with physics. The number is drawn on the server before the die leaves your hand, and the face that lands up is always that number.

    Pick a die and roll
    server--
    • check
    • damage
    • critical: a 10 explodes and adds another die
    • fumble: a 1 rolls another die and subtracts it

    Try it

    Line of sight and cover

    Walls, closed doors and anything that stops a bullet block line of sight. Ordinary glass and fences don't. The server rejects any shot at a target behind cover, and cover has hit points: you can shoot it to pieces.

    Move the red target: drag, tap a square or use the arrow keys. Click the door, or press Enter, to open or close it.

    Map

    Fog of war

    The map reveals itself around the characters as they move. What is still under the fog never reaches your screen.

    Combat

    A clock on every turn

    Two minutes to act. The clock restarts with each thing you do; sit still and the turn moves on.

    Downtime

    Days off

    On a day off each character gets a corner of the map and a private scene with the GM. Share it with the crew if you feel like it.

    Table privacy

    Only what you can see

    The server sends each player only what they are allowed to see. Whatever the GM hides on other people's sheets, secret rolls and unseen NPCs never reach your browser.

    05 · How it works

    Three steps to the first scene.

    1. Open the table

      The campaign is created and a link goes out to the group. Players install nothing.

    2. Build the crew

      Each player builds a character step by step, life story included, and can lock it with a PIN.

    3. Play

      The AI Game Master runs the session. Everything is saved for next time.
